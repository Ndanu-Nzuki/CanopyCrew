## PAGE 1 — THE PROBLEM, OUR PURPOSE AND OUR USER
### 🌱 1. The Opportunity
Imagine a group of neighbors in São Paulo who want to turn a neglected urban space into a thriving green area. They have enthusiasm, willing volunteers, and a shared vision—but where do they begin? Which plants are suitable? Will roots damage pavement or hit electrical wires? What will it cost, and who will care for the space after planting day?
> **Problem Statement:** Community volunteers often lack a single, accessible resource that translates local ecological information into suitable planting choices, realistic project estimates, and manageable long-term care activities.
CanopyCrew is an AI-assisted urban greening planning platform that helps community volunteers identify suitable native plants, understand potential costs and environmental benefits, and organize ongoing maintenance—making community greening more accessible, informed, and sustainable, beginning with a focused pilot in São Paulo, Brazil.
### 👤 2. Meet Our User: Lucas, the Saturday Volunteer
Lucas is a fictional 32-year-old neighborhood volunteer coordinator in Pinheiros, São Paulo, working with neighbors to transform an underused 35 m² urban space into a greener environment. Motivated but not a botanist or landscape designer, he needs reliable guidance without becoming an expert:
- **Plant selection:** Understanding which native species suit the space, sunlight, soil, and nearby infrastructure.
- **Budget planning:** Estimating what the planting initiative might require financially.
- **Practical implementation:** Translating botanical information into an understandable planting plan.
- **Long-term maintenance:** Keeping volunteers engaged in caring for plants beyond the initial planting activity.
Lucas is an illustrative design persona and will be validated through user feedback.
### 💡 3. Our Value Proposition
CanopyCrew turns the desire to green a neighborhood into a practical, informed planting plan by connecting site characteristics to relevant native species, indicative resource estimates, and understandable maintenance activities. It supports early planning and decision-making and does not replace municipal approvals, site inspections, or qualified ecological advice.
### 🏛️ 4. Municipal and Ecological Foundation
The pilot draws on São Paulo municipal biodiversity and urban greening resources—including Flora Nativa Paulistana, BioSampa, and the municipal urban arborization framework—to inform native vegetation selection, infrastructure constraints, and biodiversity. Species and site constraints will be reviewed against municipal guidance before use in the recommendation engine.

## PAGE 2 — THE CANOPYCREW SOLUTION
### 📋 5. What the MVP Will Deliver
To maintain a realistic four-week scope, CanopyCrew focuses on three representative urban environments and a curated catalogue of ten native Atlantic Forest plant species, demonstrating how site characteristics shape recommendations:
| Site Archetype | Description | Primary Consideration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — Compact Sidewalk Strip | Narrow planting strip (< 1.5 m wide), compacted soil, possible overhead wires | Root characteristics, pedestrian clearance, height restrictions |
| B — Sunny Degraded Lot | Approx. 20–50 m² with direct sunlight and degraded urban soil | Resilient native vegetation and ecological restoration |
| C — Shaded Courtyard | Approx. 15–30 m² with limited sunlight and humid microclimate | Shade-tolerant plants and appropriate understory vegetation |
### 🔄 6. The Volunteer Experience
**Core Journey: Discover → Select → Plan → Care**
- **Discover:** Lucas identifies the sample planting environment that most closely resembles his community’s space.
- **Select:** CanopyCrew presents relevant native plants from a curated catalogue, with suitability checks based on defined environmental and physical constraints.
- **Plan:** Lucas reviews an indicative planting concept, seedling cost estimate, and projected canopy coverage to understand the proposed transformation.
- **Care:** The system provides understandable maintenance guidance, covering initial establishment and recurring longer-term activities.
### 🧩 7. Connecting User Problems to Product Features
| Problem | MVP Feature | Expected Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Choosing unsuitable plants | Rules-based site compatibility | More informed planting decisions |
| Difficulty identifying native vegetation | Ten-species curated catalogue | Accessible, locally grounded ecological information |
| Uncertainty about costs and results | Cost and canopy estimates with a visual concept | Clearer project planning and stakeholder discussions |
| Lack of post-planting coordination | Volunteer care guidance and recurring tasks | Better understanding of ongoing maintenance responsibilities |
### 🤖 8. The Role of AI
CanopyCrew combines structured ecological information, deterministic recommendation logic, and generative AI. The recommendation engine applies defined rules to plant suitability and calculates planning estimates from structured inputs. NVIDIA NIM generates accessible explanations and volunteer care narratives based on these validated outputs—ensuring AI communicates the plan without inventing botanical facts or overriding safety and compatibility rules.
### 🚧 9. MVP Boundaries
The initial product does not attempt comprehensive citywide site mapping, custom computer vision, native mobile applications, or unrestricted ecological design. Instead, the team will focus on demonstrating a consistent planning experience for the three agreed archetypes.

### 🎯 12. Defining Success for the Four-Week MVP
| Area | Acceptance Measure |
| --- | --- |
| Site coverage | Three defined urban site profiles |
| Plant catalogue | Ten reviewed native species with supporting data |
| Recommendation quality | Recommended plants satisfy the configured compatibility rules |
| Planning experience | User can obtain a planting concept and indicative cost/canopy estimates |
| Maintenance guidance | User receives understandable establishment and longer-term care tasks |
| Usability | A nontechnical reviewer can follow the core journey and explain the next action |
**User Validation:** The team proposes testing the core user journey with five nontechnical participants. Participants will be asked to select a site, interpret plant recommendations and planning estimates, and identify their next maintenance action. The proposed success threshold is at least four out of five participants completing the journey without assistance.
### ⚠️ 13. Risks and Considerations
- Validating species information, municipal restrictions, and keeping AI outputs grounded in verified data.
- Avoiding misleading cost/environmental claims and ensuring outputs are clear to nontechnical users.
- Protecting credentials and applying appropriate cloud security controls (actual planting requires site assessment and municipal permissions).
### 🚀 14. Looking Ahead
The immediate priority is completing and demonstrating the MVP, confirming recommendation accuracy, and gathering usability feedback. Future development could expand planting environments, native species coverage, and community participation features.
